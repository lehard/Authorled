# UI/UX of professional book-writing tools (novelists), state as of late 2026

Note on sources: most information comes from vendor pages, vendor help docs and third-party review blogs (some affiliate or competitor-written; flagged inline). Dates: Kindlepreneur Dabble review updated 2025-10-01; eesel Sudowrite review edited 2026-01-21; selfpublishing.com Campfire review 2024-08-30; Scrivener forum posts span several years. No Reddit threads were returned by search directly; complaint evidence comes from Literature & Latte forums, Capterra, G2, iMore, AlternativeTo.

## Workspace structure: binder/sidebar, corkboard/cards, outliner, inspector, timeline, plot grids

### Takeaway
The category converges on one model: a hierarchical manuscript tree (book > chapter > scene) on the left, the editor in the centre, a metadata/notes inspector on the right, and alternate "views" of the same scene objects (cards/corkboard, outline table, grid/matrix of plotlines x scenes, timeline). The key borrowable idea is "one object, many views": a scene created in the manuscript automatically appears as a card in the plot grid (Dabble) or as a row in the outliner (Scrivener).

### Cited Findings
- Scrivener: corkboard, outliner and "Edit Scrivenings" (stitched view of several documents) are switched by one toolbar control over the same selection; corkboard cards show synopsis or text preview and can be reordered; outliner shows chapters/scenes with columns for status, POV, word count, subplots — [L&L blog (Scrivener 2.0, old)](https://www.literatureandlatte.com/blog/scrivener-2-0-coming-soon-no-really); [Indie Author Magazine](https://app.indieauthormagazine.com/?p=2885)
- Scrivener inspector (right panel) holds synopsis, notes, keywords, snapshots, labels, status, bookmarks, metadata, comments — [Indie Author Magazine](https://app.indieauthormagazine.com/?p=2885); [MobileRead wiki](https://wiki.mobileread.com/wiki/Scrivener)
- Capterra summary: novelists value Scrivener's binder, corkboard, split view, labels and notes — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/180597/Scrivener/reviews/Capterra___1577970/)
- Dabble: Plot Grid with plot lines, plot points and labels; arbitrary columns; creating a scene in the manuscript auto-creates a matching card in the Plot Grid; colour-coded labels on scene cards; can plot per-character lines — [Dabble features](https://www.dabblewriter.com/features); [Kindlepreneur, upd. 2025-10](https://kindlepreneur.com/dabble-writer/); [Scribecount](https://scribecount.com/author-resource/writing-tools-for-authors/dabble-writing-app-for-authors)
- Dabble also has "Story Notes" (characters, worldbuilding, research links), sticky notes, comments — [Kindlepreneur](https://kindlepreneur.com/dabble-writer/)
- Novelcrafter: Plan section with Grid, Matrix (spreadsheet-like structure overview) and Outline views — [chapter.pub review](https://blog.chapter.pub/novelcrafter-review/); [Jenova review, Apr 2026](https://www.jenova.ai/en/resources/novelcrafter)
- Reedsy Studio: planning area with storyboard/scene cards; toggle between a grid view (visual storyboard) and list view (spreadsheet-like); outlining is a paid add-on (~$7.99/mo per one review) — [The Write Practice](https://thewritepractice.com/reedsy-studio-review/)
- Plottr: timeline grid of plotlines x chapters; users praise it for laying out arcs and POVs, one uses it to plan a 30-book series — [Capterra Plottr reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/264561/Plottr/reviews/?page=3)
- Campfire: modular workspace of 17 modules (Characters, Manuscript, Locations, Maps, Research, Timeline, Arcs, Relationships, Encyclopedia, Magic, Species, Cultures, Items, Systems, Languages, Religions, Philosophies); nested interactive maps; relationship charts; multiple overlapping timelines; pay-per-module pricing — [selfpublishing.com, 2024-08](https://selfpublishing.com/campfire-writing-review/)
- Comparison (competitor-authored): Plottr stronger for chronological event tracking, Campfire stronger for worldbuilding and cross-linked modules — [chapter.pub](https://blog.chapter.pub/plottr-vs-campfire/)
- Sudowrite: Canvas, a drag-and-drop board/mind-map of connected story cards (plot points, arcs, twists); one reviewer says it fits mid-sized projects better than sprawling series; Beats retired 2025-03-31 and converted into Scenes — [eesel, 2026-01](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/sudowrite-review); [storytellingdb](https://storytellingdb.com/sudowrite-review/); [Sudowrite help glossary](https://write.helpkit.so/getting-started/dQph1snuwbfMWG9wRjsNug/glossary/1Symu5y4wtu65nQVYHjhxa)
- Manuskript (free, open source): hierarchical outliner, distraction-free editor, character management, plot tools, Snowflake-method support, "fiction" vs "simple" project modes — [Linux Magazine](https://www.linux-magazine.com/index.php/Online/Features/Write-a-Novel-with-Open-Source-Tools/(language)/eng-US); [Linux Mint community](https://community.linuxmint.com/software/view/manuskript)
- bibisco: character-centric; praised as best interface of open-source group but "insists users do things its way"; Objects and Timeline only in paid version; weak location tools (review is several years old) — [Linux Magazine](https://www.linuxpromagazine.com/Online/Features/Write-a-Novel-with-Open-Source-Tools)
- Atticus: strength is formatting print/ebook; book-level goal tracking and word count; no evidence of corkboard/storyboard; $147 lifetime — [Reedsy (promotional, competitor)](https://reedsy.com/studio/resources/atticus-review/); [The Write Practice](https://thewritepractice.com/book-writing-software-atticus-review/)
- Russian-language market: compilations recommend Scrivener, yWriter, Manuskript, bibisco, novelWriter, Obsidian; described pattern is "manuscript split into scenes and chapters, character cards, notes and draft versions alongside" — [toolfox.ru](https://toolfox.ru/services/rabota-s-tekstom/programmy-dlya-pisatelej); [toolfox novelWriter](https://toolfox.ru/services/s/novelwriter); [Lifehacker.ru, Jan 2024](https://lifehacker.ru/10-instrumentov-dlya-avtorov/)
- Litres Самиздат (Литрес Авторы): convenient constructor for the work's header/card, but a "very basic text editor with extremely scant functionality"; cover can be picked from presets or AI-generated from the annotation — [Author.Today post, platform review](https://author.today/post/567835); [Litres selfpub](https://www.litres.ru/selfpub/)
- Author.Today: praised for convenient, easy-to-navigate site interface; oriented to serial web fiction with chapter monetisation and subscriptions — [Author.Today post](https://author.today/post/720823)
- Russian writers on Author.Today share personal tool picks, e.g. ZenWriter chosen for typewriter sound and simple interface — [Author.Today «Софт для писателей»](https://author.today/post/23888)

### Inferences
- Borrow for Authorled: left tree of the 22 methodology documents grouped by stage (e.g. Фундамент / Мир / Персонажи / Структура / Черновики / Финал) with status badges (не начат / черновик ИИ / на согласовании / утверждён) is the direct analogue of Scrivener's binder + label/status columns.
- "One object, many views" (Dabble scene -> plot card; Scrivener corkboard/outliner over one selection): scene outline and beat-map documents should be the same data rendered as list, cards and grid (plotlines x scenes), not separate documents to keep in sync.
- Right-hand inspector pattern maps well to an "AI questions / options / approval" panel beside the document, keeping the centre for the author's text.
- Russian platforms (Litres, Author.Today) are publishing venues with weak editors, not planning tools; there is no strong native Russian competitor in structured writing workspaces — a gap Authorled can occupy.
- Campfire's per-module model shows writers accept a modular workspace, but 17 modules is a clutter risk; a methodology-driven sequence is more guided.

- No current (2025–2026) screenshots/docs verified for Scrivener 3 layout changes, Ulysses library/sheets, or Plottr's 2025+ UI.
- Plottr's Save the Cat template and series bible not confirmed in official docs.

## Distraction-free / focus modes, typewriter scrolling, typography, goals, streaks, snapshots

### Takeaway
Focus features are table stakes: full-screen composition mode, typewriter scrolling (current line kept centred), dimming everything but the current sentence/paragraph, and auto-hiding chrome. Goals have moved from a raw word counter to "deadline-based daily targets" that recalculate. Version history exists mostly as manual snapshots (Scrivener).

### Cited Findings
- Scrivener Composition Mode: full-screen distraction-free view; typewriter setting keeps current line centred — [Indie Author Magazine](https://app.indieauthormagazine.com/?p=2885); [Scrivener For Dummies](https://oreilly.com/library/view/scrivener-for-dummies/9781118312469)
- Scrivener Snapshots: save a document's earlier state to revert/compare; used as a record before changes or to share with an editor — [MobileRead wiki](https://wiki.mobileread.com/wiki/Scrivener); [Goodreads author blog](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/5007577-scrivener-and-the-brain?tab=author)
- Ulysses: typewriter mode keeps attention on current line/sentence/paragraph, often paired with "Mark the Current Line"; G2 users praise clean interface; main complaint is subscription cost — [Kindlepreneur Ulysses review](https://kindlepreneur.com/ulysses-review/); [Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/3278249/ulysses-13-review-for-mac-and-ios.html); [G2](https://www.g2.com/products/ulysses/reviews)
- iA Writer Focus Mode dims everything except the current lines (an older Engadget description says the three most recent lines) — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/tag/FocusMode)
- Dabble Focus Mode: side toolbars fade automatically after a short stretch of typing, return on mouse move; can be switched to a manual toggle button; the Kindlepreneur reviewer disliked auto-fade and turned it off — [Kindlepreneur, 2025-10](https://kindlepreneur.com/dabble-writer/)
- Dabble goals: set target word count and writing days (days off excluded), project- or section-level goals, auto-calculated words/day, option to exclude already-written words; NaNoWriMo sync; glossary lists "Goals and stats" incl. streaks — [Kindlepreneur](https://kindlepreneur.com/dabble-writer/); [Dabble glossary](https://www.dabblewriter.com/docs/reference/glossary); [Dabble features](https://www.dabblewriter.com/features)
- Reedsy Studio: daily word-count goal and overall manuscript target with progress tracking; advanced stats as paid upgrade — [The Write Practice](https://thewritepractice.com/reedsy-studio-review/)
- Novelcrafter "Smart Highlighting": usage frequency, AI-writing detection, dialogue-tag tracking — [Novelcrafter Codex page](https://www.novelcrafter.com/features/codex)
- NaNoWriMo shut down (announced ~31 March 2025) after financial problems and controversy including its pro-AI stance; writers migrated to other challenges — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/01/nanowrimo-shut-down-after-ai-content-moderation-scandals); [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2025/04/03/nanowrimo-closes-how-scandals-rocked-a-novel-writing-community); [ALLi](https://selfpublishingadvice.org/after-nanowrimo/)

### Inferences
- Borrow: a "режим фокуса" for the draft/final-text documents that hides the AI panel and tree, with typewriter scrolling and current-paragraph highlighting; make auto-hide opt-in (Dabble's auto-fade annoyed a reviewer).
- Borrow: deadline-based goals (target + writing days -> daily quota that recalculates) rather than streak pressure; streak mechanics are less central now that NaNoWriMo's ecosystem is gone, and NaNoWriMo-sync integrations (Dabble) have lost their partner.
- Version history is critical for an AI-assisted flow: every AI proposal and every author approval should be an automatic, labelled snapshot (Scrivener requires manual snapshots — a known gap to improve on). Showing diffs between "предложение ИИ" and "утверждённая версия" is the natural extension.
- Typography: tools praised for focus (iA Writer, Ulysses) use a single narrow text column and a calm palette; for Russian text, a serif/reading face with a ~60–75 character measure is consistent with these tools (not directly sourced).

### Gaps
- No verified current spec for page width/typography defaults in iA Writer, Ulysses or Dabble.
- Dabble streak behaviour details not retrieved.
- Version history in Dabble, Novelcrafter, Reedsy not confirmed by sources found.

## Story bibles / codex / character and location sheets linked to the manuscript

### Takeaway
Novelcrafter's Codex is the state of the art: entries for characters/places/objects with aliases are auto-detected and highlighted in the prose as you type, clickable to open the entry, with a global "every appearance" view, relations between entries, and time-dependent "progressions" tied to a scene. The codex doubles as the AI's source of truth. Sudowrite's Story Bible plays the same "AI context" role but with less manuscript linking.

### Cited Findings
- Novelcrafter Codex: names, aliases and nicknames are "instantly recognized and linked as you type" with colour highlighting; click a mention to jump to the entry; global mapping of every appearance across manuscript, chats and snippets; Preview Cards (hover cards, shown in screenshot caption); custom fields, dropdowns, custom categories; entries shareable across a series — [Novelcrafter Codex](https://www.novelcrafter.com/features/codex)
- Novelcrafter Progressions: dated additions to an entry attached to a specific scene; the AI only sees them when working at or after that scene; entry shows which scene each progression belongs to; can be tied to a specific detail field — [Novelcrafter docs: progressions](https://www.novelcrafter.com/help/docs/codex/progressions-codex-details); [track character changes](https://novelcrafter.com/help/faq/codex/track-character-changes)
- Novelcrafter Relations: when an entry is mentioned, linked entries are also pulled into the AI prompt — [Codex relations](https://www.novelcrafter.com/courses/codex-cookbook/codex-relations); [Codex context in prompting](https://www.novelcrafter.com/help/faq/ai-and-prompting/codex-context-in-prompting)
- Novelcrafter Workshop Chat: AI brainstorming chat using Codex and manuscript as context (character interviews, plot problems); Artisan tier ~$14/mo; tiers from $4 (no AI) to $20 (collaboration) — [chapter.pub](https://blog.chapter.pub/novelcrafter-review/); [Jenova, Apr 2026](https://www.jenova.ai/en/resources/novelcrafter)
- Sudowrite Story Bible: hub for genre, style, synopsis, characters, worldbuilding that the AI uses; a reviewer saw a character's noted fear of water appear unprompted in generated scenes; formerly "Story Engine"; Style and Genre are text boxes inside it — [storytellingdb, May 2025](https://storytellingdb.com/sudowrite-review/); [Stork](https://www.stork.ai/ai-tools/sudowrite); [Sudowrite help glossary](https://write.helpkit.so/getting-started/dQph1snuwbfMWG9wRjsNug/glossary/1Symu5y4wtu65nQVYHjhxa)
- Sudowrite Chapter Continuity reads across sequential documents for consistency — [Sudowrite blog / help](https://sudowrite.com/blog/sudowrite-vs-novelai-the-2025-cage-match-for-your-next-novel/)
- Campfire: Encyclopedia cross-links related entries; Characters profiles track traits and development over time; Relationships module visualises alliances/conflicts — [selfpublishing.com](https://selfpublishing.com/campfire-writing-review/)
- Plottr user wish: better character notes and linked wiki / family-tree options — [Capterra Plottr](https://www.capterra.com/p/264561/Plottr/reviews/?page=5)
- AuthorMedia Character Compendium generates a structured character reference (personality, motivation, appearance, arc, audiobook voice notes) by reading the manuscript — [AuthorMedia](https://www.authormedia.com/patron-toolbox/character-compendium/)

### Inferences
- Borrow directly: character, location and world documents (from the 22-document set) should become codex entities with aliases (critical for Russian: declension forms — «Анна/Анны/Анне/Аню» — need morphological matching, not plain string matching), auto-highlighted in scene outlines and drafts, with a hover card (shadcn HoverCard) showing key facts and a link to the full sheet.
- Progressions are a strong fit for beat maps: a character's state "as of scene N" lets the AI (and author) see arc changes without overwriting the base sheet.
- "Where is this mentioned" backlinks panel per entity helps the author check consistency after approving changes to a character.
- Make AI context explicit and visible (Novelcrafter shows what the AI sees via codex/relations); for an approval-first product, show "ИИ опирается на: Тема, Персонаж X, Мир" chips on each proposal.
- Reverse extraction (Character Compendium style: read draft -> propose sheet updates) fits Authorled's "AI proposes, author approves" loop.

### Gaps
- Exact hover-card behaviour and visual design in Novelcrafter not documented in text sources found.
- No source on how any tool handles inflected languages (Russian cases) in mention detection.

## Onboarding and templates

### Takeaway
Two onboarding archetypes: (1) blank-but-simple (Dabble: writing within minutes, no card needed) and (2) structure templates (Plottr 20+ starter plot templates; Sudowrite guided synopsis->framework flow; Manuskript Snowflake mode; Scrivener built-in tutorial project). Feature-heavy tools explicitly tell users not to learn everything at once.

### Cited Findings
- Dabble: free 14-day trial without card; reviewer was writing within minutes; help article library; simpler but less powerful than Scrivener — [Kindlepreneur](https://kindlepreneur.com/dabble-writer/)
- Plottr: over 20 Starter Templates for timelines, incl. Hero's Journey, Romancing the Beat, 12 Chapter Mystery, W Plot, 7 Point Plot Structure — [Plottr docs](https://docs.plottr.com/article/214-timeline-starter-templates)
- Scrite: Save the Cat template auto-tags each of 15 beats in the beat sheet — [Scrite docs](https://www.scrite.io/docs/userguide/templates/)
- Sudowrite: guided flow — enter synopsis, genre, character details -> generates a structured narrative framework — [Sudowrite blog](https://sudowrite.com/blog/sudowrite-vs-novelai-the-2025-cage-match-for-your-next-novel/)
- Novelcrafter getting-started guide: you don't need every feature immediately, go at your own pace; AI chat can be skipped — [Novelcrafter getting started](https://www.novelcrafter.com/help/getting-started/general/getting-started-with-novelcrafter)
- Scrivener veterans recommend doing the built-in tutorial and not trying to learn every feature first — [L&L forum](https://forum.literatureandlatte.com/t/problems-at-first/48372)
- Manuskript offers "fiction" vs "simple" modes at project creation — [Linux Magazine](https://www.linux-magazine.com/index.php/Online/Features/Write-a-Novel-with-Open-Source-Tools/(language)/eng-US)
- Campfire: free signup without card; maps module walks the user through creating a map — [selfpublishing.com](https://selfpublishing.com/campfire-writing-review/)

### Inferences
- Authorled's methodology itself is the onboarding: the first screen should be "start a project" -> first document (Тема) with the AI's first question, not an empty dashboard. Progressive disclosure: show later stages locked/greyed with clear "unlocks after X is approved".
- Offer a "simple/full" mode choice (Manuskript) or a short path vs full 22-doc path for screenwriters vs novelists, if the methodology allows.
- Plottr/Scrite show writers value named structure templates (Save the Cat, Hero's Journey); beat-map documents could offer these as AI-proposed starting options the author picks from.

### Gaps
- No detailed first-run screenshots or onboarding-step sequences found for Novelcrafter, Sudowrite, Dabble.

## Recurring user complaints

### Takeaway
Complaints cluster around: steep learning curve and feature overload (Scrivener, Novelcrafter AI setup), sync reliability (Scrivener + Dropbox, Plottr cloud per a competitor), subscription cost (Ulysses, Dabble), missing import/export (Dabble), opinionated rigid workflows (bibisco), and AI-specific issues (credits, generic prose).

### Cited Findings
- Scrivener: steep learning curve is the most common con; compile is the hardest part; some users quit ("couldn't work out how to do anything"); Mac/Windows feature gap — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/180597/Scrivener/reviews/Capterra___1577970/); [iMore forum](https://forums.imore.com/threads/how-do-mac-writers-like-ia-pro-especially-those-who-find-scrivener-difficult-to-learn.336244/); [L&L forum](https://forum.literatureandlatte.com/t/problems-at-first/48372); [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/scrivener/about)
- Scrivener iOS/Dropbox sync: recurring phantom "conflict" files; workarounds involve waiting for Dropbox and closing projects before switching devices — [L&L forum](https://forum.literatureandlatte.com/t/creates-conflict-files-when-there-are-no-conflicts/33924); [L&L forum](https://forum.literatureandlatte.com/t/sync-conflicts-detected-on-ipad-but-no-changes-on-desktop-observed/133273)
- Plottr: sync/mobile reliability problems and collaboration only on Pro (claims from competitor Chapter; treat with caution) — [chapter.pub](https://blog.chapter.pub/plottr-review/)
- Dabble: subscription cost (two years of Standard ≈ $288), no Word/Google Docs import, only Word export, no print/EPUB formatting — [Kindlepreneur](https://kindlepreneur.com/dabble-writer/)
- Ulysses: subscription is the main complaint — [Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/3278249/ulysses-13-review-for-mac-and-ios.html)
- bibisco: forces its own way of working — [Linux Magazine](https://www.linuxpromagazine.com/Online/Features/Write-a-Novel-with-Open-Source-Tools)
- Novelcrafter: bring-your-own-key AI setup is the steepest part of the learning curve; more work per chapter — [Jenova](https://www.jenova.ai/en/resources/novelcrafter); [ghostwritingsolution](https://ghostwritingsolution.com/blog/novelcrafter-review-is-it-the-most-powerful-ai-tool-for-writers/)
- Sudowrite: credit system learning curve, output can sound generic without heavy editing (review from a vendor of a competing tool) — [eesel, 2026-01](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/sudowrite-review)
- Campfire: can cost more than other tools; tilted to SF/fantasy — [selfpublishing.com](https://selfpublishing.com/campfire-writing-review/)
- Litres Самиздат editor: very scant functionality — [Author.Today](https://author.today/post/567835)

### Inferences
- A web app with server-side storage avoids the #1 desktop sync pain; still needs visible save state and conflict-free multi-tab behaviour.
- bibisco's "do it our way" criticism is a direct warning for a methodology-driven product: allow skipping/reordering documents or at least explain why the order matters, and let authors import existing material.
- Import (docx, plain text) and export (docx, at minimum) are expected even in simple tools; Dabble is criticised for lacking them.
- Keep AI cost model invisible or simple; credit systems are a friction point.

### Gaps
- No direct Reddit (r/writing, r/scrivener) threads retrieved; complaint evidence comes from forums and review aggregators.
- No user complaint data found for Author.Today's editor or Russian-specific tools.

## Design trends 2024–2026

### Takeaway
Trends: (1) story bible / codex as structured AI context with automatic linking; (2) AI as a side chat/workshop plus inline actions (Write/Expand/Describe/Rewrite) rather than full generation; (3) provenance — marking which text is human vs AI (iA Writer Authorship); (4) browser-based, cross-device apps (Dabble, Novelcrafter, Reedsy, Campfire) displacing desktop+Dropbox; (5) modular/tiered pricing; (6) cultural backlash against AI in writing communities.

### Cited Findings
- iA Writer 7 "Authorship": AI text can be marked and shown greyed out (Mac setting: coloured gradient), reference text dimmed; three categories Human / AI-generated / Reference; "Paste as ChatGPT"; local-only, not a detector — [iA Writer 7](https://ia.net/topics/ia-writer-7); [iA support: Authorship](https://ia.net/writer/support/editor/authorship?tab=iphone); [AIPressRoom](https://aipressroom.beehiiv.com/p/ia-writer-can-now-track-what-you-or-chatgpt-wrote)
- Sudowrite inline tools: Write (suggests next ~300 words, several options, author chooses), Expand, Describe (sensory details for a word), Rewrite with goals like "Show, Not Tell", "More Inner Conflict"; Feedback on arc/pacing; 1,000+ community plugins; custom Muse model — [eesel, 2026-01](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/sudowrite-review)
- Novelcrafter: AI uses Codex as "source of truth"; character interviews in chat; Smart Highlighting includes AI-writing detection — [Novelcrafter Codex](https://www.novelcrafter.com/features/codex)
- Sudowrite retired Beats (March 2025) in favour of Scenes, simplifying structure — [Sudowrite help glossary](https://write.helpkit.so/getting-started/dQph1snuwbfMWG9wRjsNug/glossary/1Symu5y4wtu65nQVYHjhxa)
- Litres offers AI-generated covers from the annotation — [Author.Today review](https://author.today/post/567835)
- NaNoWriMo's pro-AI stance contributed to board resignations and lost community support before its 2025 closure — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/01/nanowrimo-shut-down-after-ai-content-moderation-scandals)

### Inferences
- Authorled's "AI asks, author approves" stance aligns with the provenance trend; borrow iA's visual distinction: AI-proposed text visually distinct (muted/tinted) until the author accepts or rewrites it, and record authorship per document.
- Present AI output as several options to choose from (Sudowrite Write), with explicit accept/edit/reject, rather than auto-inserting.
- Named rewrite intents (Sudowrite) are a good pattern for revision documents: preset buttons in Russian («Показать, а не рассказать», «Усилить конфликт»).
- Given community hostility to AI, messaging should emphasise author ownership and control; avoid AI ghostwriting framing.

- No authoritative 2026 design-trend survey or design case study/teardown of these tools was found.
- No confirmed info on Scrivener 4, Ulysses 2025–2026 releases, or Plottr/Campfire 2026 redesigns.
